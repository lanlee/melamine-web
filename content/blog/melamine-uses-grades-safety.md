# Melamine: 6 Uses, 3 Purity Grades, and 5 Buying Checks

**Author:** Guangdong HanCheng Material

**Date:** 2026-09-08

## TL;DR

Melamine is a nitrogen-rich organic compound (C3H6N6, CAS 108-78-1) made from urea. Combined with formaldehyde it becomes the resin behind laminates, dinnerware, adhesives, concrete additives, flame retardants and paper coatings. It is approved for food-contact tableware but never for microwaving or direct addition to food.

## Key Takeaways

- Melamine is a raw material, not a finished product. Its value comes from what it becomes after reacting with formaldehyde: melamine-formaldehyde (MF) resin.
- Purity is the biggest quality variable, and it splits into 3 grades. Premium is 99.8% minimum, industrial is about 99.5%. Insist on a Certificate of Analysis for every lot.
- Melamine tableware is FDA-approved for food contact, but heating acidic food on it increases migration. Never microwave it.
- Regulators disagree on the tolerable daily intake: EFSA set 0.2 mg/kg body weight, the US FDA 0.063 mg/kg. Design to the stricter one if you sell into both markets.
- China produced about 75% of the world's 1.6 million tonnes of melamine in 2020, so freight, Incoterms and lot-level paperwork matter as much as unit price.

## What Is Melamine, Exactly?

Melamine is a white, odourless crystalline solid with the formula C3H6N6 and the IUPAC name 1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triamine. It is a trimer of cyanamide built on a 1,3,5-triazine ring, first synthesised by Justus von Liebig in 1834. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamine)

If you buy it, these are the numbers that actually matter:

| Property | Value | Why a buyer cares |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical formula | C3H6N6 | Confirms you are comparing like with like across suppliers |
| CAS number | 108-78-1 | The unambiguous identifier for customs and MSDS documents |
| Molar mass | 126.123 g/mol | Basis for stoichiometry when you formulate MF resin |
| Nitrogen content | 66% by mass | Drives both resin performance and its abuse as a protein-test fraud |
| Melting point | 343 °C (decomposes) | It does not melt cleanly; it sublimes and breaks down |
| Water solubility | 3,240 mg/L at 20 °C | Limits how it can be dosed into aqueous systems |
| Appearance | White crystalline solid | Discolouration is your first visible red flag |

The last row is the one buyers underestimate. Melamine should arrive as a clean white free-flowing powder. Any yellow or grey cast usually means contamination or overheated stock, and it will show up in your finished laminate or moulded part.

> **Quotable:** Melamine is a commodity in chemistry but not in quality control. The powder looks identical across suppliers; the paperwork discipline does not.

## Where Melamine Comes From

Modern melamine is made from urea, not from calcium cyanamide as it was historically. The overall reaction is:

**6 (NH2)2CO → C3H6N6 + 6 NH3 + 3 CO2**

Molten urea is converted either in a catalysed gas-phase process over a fluidised bed with hot ammonia, or in a high-pressure liquid-phase process. The off-gas is rich in ammonia, so melamine plants are usually integrated with the upstream urea plant and the ammonia is recycled. Major technology licensors include Orascom, BASF and Eurotecnica. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamine)

This integration is why melamine pricing tracks urea and natural gas or coal. When you see a melamine quote move without any change in melamine supply, the cause is almost always feedstock.

## The 6 Industrial Uses of Melamine

Melamine is almost never used alone. It is a building block that becomes useful once it is condensed with formaldehyde into MF resin. These are the six applications that account for essentially all industrial demand.

### 1. Decorative laminates and furniture surfaces

MF resin is the hard, clear, scratch- and heat-resistant skin on high-pressure laminates, laminate flooring, cabinet carcasses and worktops. This is the largest single end use worldwide.

### 2. Dinnerware and kitchenware

Compression-moulded MF resin produces plates, bowls, trays and cups that are shatter-resistant, lightweight and dishwasher-safe. The FDA has approved melamine for use in manufacturing tableware and cooking utensils. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/economically-motivated-adulteration-food-fraud/melamine-tableware-questions-and-answers)

### 3. Coatings and wood adhesives

Melamine crosslinkers harden baking enamels and thermoset coatings, and MF resin binds plywood, particleboard and MDF. Crosslink density here is what determines stain and water resistance.

### 4. Concrete superplasticizers

Sulfonated melamine formaldehyde (SMF) acts as a high-range water reducer in concrete, letting precast and ready-mix producers cut water content while keeping workability.

### 5. Flame retardants

Melamine and its salts are added to paints, plastics, polyurethane foam and paper. It works in the gas phase by releasing nitrogen, diluting combustible gases. Melamine-derived fibres such as Basofil are used in heat-protective clothing. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamine)

### 6. Paper and textile treatment

MF resins give wet-strength to paper and crease resistance to cellulosic fabrics, which is why they appear in everything from filter media to work shirts.

**One use that is not on this list, deliberately:** melamine is not approved for direct addition to human food or animal feed in the United States. It has been used to fake protein test results precisely because it is 66% nitrogen by mass, which defeats Kjeldahl and Dumas protein assays. The 2008 Chinese dairy scandal that sickened nearly 300,000 infants came from this abuse. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamine)

## 3 Melamine Purity Grades Explained

Grades differ on minimum purity, and the difference is mostly about colour stability and rejection rate in colour-critical work. This is how we grade melamine powder at HanCheng:

| Grade | Minimum purity | Typical applications |
|---|---|---|
| Premium | 99.8% minimum | High-pressure decorative laminates, white or light-coloured moulded goods, food-contact dinnerware, clear coatings |
| Industrial | 99.5% minimum | Concrete superplasticizers, flame retardants, paper and textile treatment, wood adhesives |
| Technical | 99.0% minimum | Non-critical internal processes; uncommon in international trade |

Our 99.8% melamine powder is the grade we ship most of, and it is the one we recommend by default for laminates and dinnerware. Moving to industrial grade saves money, but it raises the odds of off-white finished product. If colour is a specification on your end product, stay at premium grade. Full data is on our [product specifications](https://www.hanchengmaterial.com/#products).

The document that proves a grade is the Certificate of Analysis, not the brochure. A COA should list assay/purity, moisture, ash, pH, and colour or appearance for the specific lot you are buying.

## Is Melamine Safe?

For its intended uses, yes, with one clear rule. Melamine is approved in the United States for manufacturing cooking utensils, plates, plastic products, paper, paperboard and industrial coatings, and is not approved for direct addition to food or feed. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/economically-motivated-adulteration-food-fraud/melamine-tableware-questions-and-answers)

The risk comes from migration: residual melamine from the resin reaction can move slowly out of finished plastic into food. The variables are heat, acidity and time.

| Benchmark | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EU tolerable daily intake | 0.2 mg/kg body weight per day | EFSA, 2010 |
| US FDA tolerable daily intake | 0.063 mg/kg body weight per day | FDA safety assessment |
| FDA acceptable level, foods other than infant formula | 2,500 parts per billion | FDA |
| Codex maximum level, powdered infant formula | 1 mg/kg | Codex Alimentarius |
| Codex maximum level, other foods | 2.5 mg/kg | Codex Alimentarius |
| Measured migration into cold orange juice, about 15 minutes | Under 10 parts per billion | FDA |

That last row is the reassuring one: FDA's realistic-use test found migration roughly 250 times below its own 2,500 ppb level, and only 3 of 19 commercially available plates and cups showed migration into acidic food at all. The concerning condition is heat plus acid — the FDA's exaggerated test held food at 160 °F for two hours, and that is where migration climbed.

**The rule that matters in practice:** never use melamine dinnerware in a microwave, and never use it to hold hot, highly acidic food for long periods. Heat it in ceramic or in cookware labelled microwave-safe, then serve on melamine. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/economically-motivated-adulteration-food-fraud/melamine-tableware-questions-and-answers)

For acute toxicity, melamine is roughly comparable to table salt by weight: rat oral LD50 is about 3,248 to 3,850 mg/kg. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamine) The hazard that actually caused human harm was deliberate adulteration of milk, not ordinary use of finished goods.

If you manufacture for both the EU and the US, note the TDI gap. EFSA's 0.2 mg/kg and the FDA's 0.063 mg/kg differ by more than a factor of three, because EFSA tightened its figure over uncertainties in the Chinese infant data. Formulate and test against the stricter limit, not the convenient one. [EFSA](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.2903/j.efsa.2010.1573)

## How to Buy Melamine Powder: 5 Checks Before You Pay

If you are sourcing for production, this is the sequence that prevents expensive mistakes.

### Check 1. Match the grade to your end product first

Write the minimum purity into your purchase order — 99.8% for laminates, dinnerware and anything colour-critical; 99.5% for concrete additives, flame retardants and adhesives. Grade is a specification, not a marketing tier.

### Check 2. Get COA, MSDS and third-party test reports before you negotiate price

Ask for all three up front, not after the order is confirmed. Every batch we ship goes out with full COA, MSDS and SGS test reports, and any supplier who cannot produce them for the specific lot is telling you something.

### Check 3. Verify purity by lot, not by brochure

Brochure purity is a target; COA purity is a measurement. Ask whether the assay is from the plant's own lab or an independent one, and request the report for the exact lot number on your packing list.

### Check 4. Fix MOQ, packaging and Incoterms before you talk price

Bag size, pallet configuration, container loadability and whether you are buying FOB, CIF or DAP will move your landed cost more than a small unit-price discount will. Confirm which port the cargo leaves from.

### Check 5. Run incoming QC before you release final payment

Check appearance, moisture and assay against the COA on receipt. A one-page incoming test catches a mixed lot before it becomes a full production run of off-spec laminate.

## Why Most Melamine Comes From China

Melamine is a urea-integrated commodity, and urea capacity is concentrated where natural gas and coal are cheap. That is why the supply map looks the way it does.

- Global melamine production reached 1.6 million tonnes in 2020, and about 75% of it occurred in China. [Environmental Science & Technology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11017250/)
- China's domestic melamine capacity was 2.31 million tonnes in 2024, with output of 1.642 million tonnes, up 9.8% year on year; by August 2025 capacity had reached 2.42 million tonnes. [SunSirs](https://www.sunsirs.com/uk/detail_news-27019.html)

Two practical consequences for buyers. First, Chinese export volume is large enough that lead times and container availability can move with global shipping conditions, so hold buffer stock for peak season. Second, because so much supply is export-oriented, the real differentiator between suppliers is documentation and consistency, not chemistry — the melamine itself is broadly similar, and the paperwork discipline is not.

## Melamine, Ceramic or Polypropylene?

If you are specifying a material rather than a raw powder, this is the short version.

| | Melamine (MF) | Ceramic | Polypropylene |
|---|---|---|---|
| Break resistance | Excellent | Poor | Excellent |
| Dishwasher safe | Yes | Yes | Varies |
| Microwave safe | No | Yes | Only if labelled |
| Heat tolerance | Moderate | High | Low |
| Cost per unit | Low | High | Lowest |
| Surface feel | Hard, gloss | Hard, gloss | Soft, flexible |

Melamine wins on durability and cost where food is served cold or warm and handled heavily: cafeterias, outdoor dining, poolside service and children's tableware. It loses where food goes straight from the microwave or oven.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is melamine made from?

Modern melamine is made from urea. Molten urea is heated in either a catalysed gas-phase or high-pressure liquid-phase process, yielding melamine plus ammonia and carbon dioxide, with the ammonia recycled back into the urea plant.

### Is melamine safe to eat off?

Yes, for normal use. The FDA approves melamine for manufacturing tableware, and its testing found migration below 10 parts per billion into cold acidic food — about 250 times lower than its own 2,500 ppb acceptable level. Do not microwave it and do not hold hot acidic food on it.

### Why can't you microwave melamine?

Heat drives residual melamine out of the resin and into food, and the effect is strongest with acidic food. The FDA states plainly that food and drink should not be heated on melamine dinnerware in a microwave oven.

### What is the difference between melamine and melamine-formaldehyde resin?

Melamine is the raw powder. Melamine-formaldehyde resin is what you get after it condenses with formaldehyde into a hard thermosetting plastic. Dinnerware, laminates and adhesives are all made from the resin, not from the powder.

### What purity of melamine should I buy?

Use 99.8% minimum for decorative laminates, white or light-coloured moulded goods, food-contact dinnerware and clear coatings. Use about 99.5% for concrete superplasticizers, flame retardants, paper and textile treatment and wood adhesives.

### Is melamine the same as plastic?

No. Melamine is a specific organic compound. Products described as "melamine" are usually made from melamine-formaldehyde resin, a thermoset that is harder and more heat-tolerant than commodity plastics such as polypropylene.
