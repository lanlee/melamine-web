## What is melamine formaldehyde?

A thermosetting amino resin: once cured, heat will not remelt it. The recipe is fixed even in food-contact law — [US FDA rules define melamine-formaldehyde resins as 1 mole of melamine reacted with not more than 3 moles of formaldehyde in water solution](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/177.1460). That melamine is the powder you buy; our [melamine powder guide](https://www.hanchengmaterial.com/blog/melamine-powder) covers specifying it.

## How does it cure? Two stages, one temperature threshold

| Stage | What happens | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Methylolation | Formaldehyde adds up to six hydroxymethyl groups to the ring | Alkaline pH | Still-processable prepolymer |
| 2. Crosslinking | They condense into methylene and ether bridges between rings | Above 160 °C | Permanently infusible thermoset |

Calorimetry shows the split: [two exothermic peaks mark a two-step cure, with reversible demethylolation dominating between 140–160 °C and crosslinking taking over above 160 °C](https://www.nature.com/articles/pj2012162). Under-cured parts trap free monomer — the very thing that later migrates — so cure temperature is a moulder's biggest lever.

## Which safety limits actually apply?

Two numbers matter. The EU's migration limit for melamine in food is 2.5 mg/kg — cut from 30 mg/kg in 2011 — with formaldehyde at 15 mg/kg. The US instead caps chloroform-soluble extractives at 0.5 mg per square inch of food-contact surface.

Stress tests show why use conditions matter. A German BfR study found [melamine released into boiling 3% acetic acid exceeded the EU limit by at least a factor of 50, while release into plain water was only 1–4% of that acid value](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7463570/); the authors concluded melamine resins are unsuited to cooking. Read plainly: serving food on melamine dinnerware is the intended use; boiling it is not, and melamine tableware is [not microwave safe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamine_resin).

## Key Takeaways

- Melamine formaldehyde cures in two stages; above 160 °C crosslinking dominates and the part becomes permanently infusible.
- The 5 uses are high-pressure laminates, molded dinnerware, wood adhesives, surface coatings and foam.
- Food-contact law fixes the recipe at ≤3 moles formaldehyde per mole of melamine; the EU migration limit is 2.5 mg/kg.
- Use conditions decide migration as much as resin quality: avoid heat plus acidity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is melamine formaldehyde the same as melamine resin?

Yes. Melamine resin is the shortened trade name for the same melamine-formaldehyde thermoset.

### What is the difference between melamine and melamine formaldehyde?

Melamine is the raw white crystalline powder — 99.8% purity in our grades. Melamine formaldehyde is the cured resin that forms once it reacts.

### Can melamine formaldehyde go in the microwave?

No. Melamine tableware is [not microwave safe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melamine_resin); heat with acidic food is what drives monomer release.

### What molar ratio is used to make melamine formaldehyde resin?

Food-contact rules cap it at 3 moles of formaldehyde per mole of melamine; published curing studies use that same 1:3 ratio.
