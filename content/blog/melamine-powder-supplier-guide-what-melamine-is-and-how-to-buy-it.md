# Melamine Powder Supplier Guide: What Melamine Is and How to Buy It

**Published:** 2026-08-28 14:08

## Introduction

If you are here, you are probably searching for melamine powder because your business needs it — for laminates, tableware, adhesives, coatings, or another production process. You may know roughly what it does, but you want a clear, honest explanation before you spend money on it.

This guide gives you that explanation in plain language. We cover what melamine powder actually is, where it comes from, what it is used for, how its grades and specifications work, whether it is safe, and — most importantly — exactly how to buy it from a supplier without getting burned.

### The short answer first

**Melamine powder is a white, crystalline industrial chemical (CAS 108-78-1, formula C₃H₆N₆) made from urea. It is not a finished material on its own — it is a raw material that manufacturers combine with formaldehyde to produce melamine-formaldehyde (MF) resin, which becomes everything from laminate countertops and dinner plates to wood adhesives, coatings, and flame retardants.**

If you buy melamine powder, you are buying the building block. Choosing the right grade and the right supplier determines whether your end product is consistent, compliant, and profitable. That is what this guide helps you do.

## Main Content

### What Is Melamine Powder?

Melamine — also called 1,3,5-triazine-2,4,6-triamine or cyanuramide — is an organic compound belonging to a family of chemicals called *amino resins*. Commercially, it is produced from urea, which is itself made from natural gas and ammonia.

**How it is made.** In modern factories, molten urea is fed into a reactor and heated to roughly 350–450 °C under high pressure (about 70–150 bar). The urea converts into melamine, releasing ammonia and carbon dioxide that are usually recycled back to the urea plant. The reaction looks like this:

> 6 urea → melamine + 6 ammonia + 3 carbon dioxide

The molten melamine is then cooled into a white crystalline powder. China produces roughly half of the world's melamine, which is why most global buyers source it from Chinese manufacturers and exporters — proximity to urea feedstock and low production costs keep Chinese product price-competitive worldwide.

**A quick note on nitrogen.** Melamine is about 66% nitrogen by weight. That high nitrogen content is why melamine makes effective, halogen-free flame retardants — and it is also the reason melamine was infamously misused as a fake "protein" in the 2008 milk scandal. That history is why buyers and regulators treat melamine carefully today, and why the safety section below matters.

### What Is Melamine Powder Used For?

Melamine powder itself is rarely used directly. It is almost always reacted with formaldehyde to build **melamine-formaldehyde (MF) resin**, a thermosetting plastic that is hard, glossy, heat-resistant, moisture-resistant, and scratch-resistant. That single resin family serves many industries:

| Application | What it is used in |
|---|---|
| **Laminates & panels** | High-pressure (HPL) and low-pressure (CPL) decorative laminates — countertops, laminate flooring, wall panels, furniture surfaces, and cabinetry. This is the single largest use. |
| **Tableware & kitchenware** | Melamine dinner plates, bowls, trays, and hotel ware, produced from *melamine molding compound* (see below). |
| **Adhesives & wood bonding** | Melamine-urea-formaldehyde (MUF) adhesives for plywood, particleboard, MDF, and engineered wood — adding moisture resistance that plain urea resin lacks. |
| **Coatings** | Amino resin cross-linkers in industrial surface coatings, can coatings, and powder coatings. |
| **Flame retardants** | Melamine itself, melamine cyanurate, and melamine polyphosphate — halogen-free flame retardants used in polyurethane foams, polyamides, and intumescent coatings. |
| **Paper treatment** | Wet-strength resins and paper surface treatment. |
| **Other uses** | Textile finishing, concrete superplasticizers (melamine sulfonate condensates), and melamine foam (the "magic eraser" cleaning sponges). |

**Important: melamine powder vs. melamine molding compound.** Buyers often confuse these two products, and they are *not* the same thing:

- **Melamine powder** (raw chemical, CAS 108-78-1) is the pure feedstock for making resin. It is not used directly to make tableware.
- **Melamine molding compound (MMC)** is a formulated powder: melamine-formaldehyde resin mixed with cellulose filler, curing agents, lubricants, and color pigments. It is the material that is actually compression-molded into plates and bowls. MMC has its own HS code (3909.20) and its own supplier selection criteria.

If you are making tableware, you buy molding compound. If you are making resin, laminates, or adhesives, you buy raw melamine powder. Confirm which one you need before you start comparing quotes.

### Melamine Powder Grades and Specifications

Most industrial melamine powder is sold on purity, and the market standard is **≥99.8%**. You will also see 99.5% (economy/industrial), 99.9% (premium), and occasionally higher grades for specialty use.

A trustworthy supplier quotes more than purity. The parameters that actually decide how the powder behaves in your process are:

| Parameter | What it controls | Typical industrial spec |
|---|---|---|
| Purity (assay) | Resin reaction stability, batch-to-batch consistency, end-product quality | ≥99.8% |
| Moisture | Caking, storage stability, reaction control | ≤0.1% |
| Ash content | Inorganic impurities | ≤0.03% |
| pH (10% slurry) | Resin reaction behavior | 7.5–9.5 |
| Color (Pt-Co/APHA) | Surface appearance of finished goods | ≤20 Hazen |
| Turbidity | Filtration behavior, surface defects | ≤20 |
| Particle size | Dissolution speed, reaction behavior | Typically 160–200 mesh; fine grades ≥95% below 45 μm |

Ask any supplier for the exact numbers in their **batch-level Certificate of Analysis (COA)**, not just a brochure. A supplier who publishes tight tolerance windows is a supplier with process control; wide or vague windows are a warning sign.

### Is Melamine Powder Safe?

This question deserves a careful, honest answer, because there are two very different things being asked about.

**1. Raw melamine powder as an industrial chemical.** Raw melamine is a low-toxicity industrial chemical, but it is *not* a food ingredient and must never be added to food or animal feed. Handle it like any industrial powder: it is mildly irritating to eyes and skin, and finely dispersed melamine dust can form a combustible dust cloud in the right conditions, so keep work areas ventilated, avoid dust accumulation, and use the same basic PPE you would for any chemical powder (dust mask, goggles, gloves). Stored sealed in a cool, dry place, it has a typical shelf life of about 24 months.

**2. Finished melamine tableware and food contact.** When high-purity melamine is properly converted into MF resin and molded into tableware, the finished product is a stable, cross-linked plastic that is regulated and approved for food contact in major markets:

- **United States:** The FDA regulates melamine-formaldehyde resins for food contact under **21 CFR 177.1460** and has established an acceptable level of 2.5 parts per million (2,500 ppb) for melamine in food (other than infant formula).
- **European Union:** EU food-contact rules set a specific migration limit (SML) for melamine of **2.5 mg/kg food** (tightened in 2011), and certified products are widely sold across Europe.
- **Practical rules:** Do not microwave melamine tableware, do not cook in it, and avoid storing very hot or highly acidic foods in it for long periods. Certified food-contact tableware is sold as **A5-grade molding compound**; cheaper A1/A3 urea-based compounds are *not* food-safe and should never be used for food contact.

The bottom line: melamine is safe to use when it is bought for the right purpose, used correctly, and supplied by a company that documents what they sell.

### How to Buy Melamine Powder: A Step-by-Step Guide

Buying a commodity chemical as a B2B buyer is different from buying a finished product. The process below is the one experienced procurement teams follow — it protects you from the three biggest risks in this market: wrong product, inconsistent quality, and unreliable suppliers.

**Step 1 — Confirm which product you need.** Raw melamine powder (for resin, laminates, adhesives, coatings) or melamine molding compound (for tableware)? If you are not sure, ask a supplier's technical contact — a good one will tell you honestly rather than sell you the wrong item.

**Step 2 — Write down your specs and volume.** Purity, particle size, moisture limit, packaging (25 kg bags vs. jumbo bags), and how much you need per order and per year. This becomes your RFQ (request for quotation).

**Step 3 — Request samples before bulk.** Any credible supplier will send 1–25 kg samples (usually free, shipping at your cost). Test the sample in your actual process — dissolution, reactivity, color, molding behavior. Do not skip this step, even if the price looks good.

**Step 4 — Vet the supplier's documentation.** Ask for:

- **Batch-specific COA** with lot numbers and test methods (not a generic one)
- **SDS/MSDS** matching the current transport and hazard rules
- **Technical data sheet (TDS)**
- **ISO 9001** quality certification (and ideally ISO 14001)
- **REACH / RoHS** compliance statements if you ship to the EU
- Third-party inspection reports (SGS, Bureau Veritas) if you want extra assurance

**Step 5 — Get a complete, itemized quote.** A serious quote answers, in writing: price per ton (and which Incoterm — FOB, CIF, or EXW), MOQ, lead time, packaging, and payment terms (typically T/T or L/C). "Price per ton" alone is meaningless without the Incoterm and port.

**Step 6 — Confirm shipping, HS code, and customs.** Raw melamine powder is classified under **HS code 2933.61**; melamine molding compound/resin powder is **3909.20**. Your supplier should provide the commercial invoice, packing list, COA, and origin documentation that match the customs declaration. A 20-foot container holds roughly 20–24 metric tons in 25 kg bags.

**Step 7 — Start with a trial order, then scale.** Place a first order that matches your near-term needs, verify quality and delivery reliability, and only then lock in a repeat-order agreement with stable pricing and priority supply.

### Understanding Melamine Powder Prices

Melamine is a commodity, and its price moves with its raw materials. Roughly 70% of production cost is urea and energy, so when natural gas, methanol, or urea prices rise, melamine prices follow. Prices also swing with supply (plant shutdowns and restarts in China) and seasonality in construction and furniture markets.

As a directional reference, **domestic Chinese ex-factory prices in 2026 have ranged between roughly 5,650 and 9,675 RMB per ton (about $780 to $1,340)**, with significant swings through the year. Export FOB quotes depend heavily on grade, quantity, port, and negotiation — so always ask for a current, itemized quote rather than relying on any published number, including this one.

What a good quote protects you from: price changes after commitment, hidden documentation fees, and freight surprises. Get the full landed-cost picture — price, freight, insurance, and duties — before you compare suppliers.

## Key Takeaways

- **Melamine powder (CAS 108-78-1) is a white crystalline chemical made from urea** — a raw material, not a finished product. Its main job is reacting with formaldehyde to form melamine-formaldehyde resin.
- **It is everywhere:** laminates and countertops, melamine tableware, wood adhesives, coatings, flame retardants, paper treatment, and melamine foam. Laminates are the largest end use.
- **Standard industrial grade is ≥99.8% purity**, but moisture, ash, pH, color, and particle size matter just as much. Always request a batch-specific COA, not a generic one.
- **Raw melamine powder is not food; finished A5 melamine tableware is food-contact regulated** (FDA 21 CFR 177.1460; EU SML 2.5 mg/kg). Handle the powder as an industrial chemical with normal PPE and dust control.
- **Buy in seven steps:** confirm the product, define specs, sample first, vet documentation, get an itemized quote, check HS code/shipping, and start with a trial order.
- **Prices are volatile and tied to urea/energy costs** — treat any published price as directional and request a current quote with a clear Incoterm.
- **Choose a supplier on consistency and documentation, not price alone.** A responsive partner with verifiable quality is worth more than the cheapest per-ton rate.
